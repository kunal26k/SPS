How We Teach

The SPS Learning Framework.

Our pedagogy isn't an imported model with a marketing name. It's a quiet set of habits we have refined since 2001 — articulated here so parents, students and teachers can hold us to it.

Our Framework

Understand. Explore. Express. Refine.

Every unit at SPS — whether it is Class III on the water cycle or Class XII on integral calculus — moves through four deliberate stages. Children first understand the why, then explore through hands-on work, then express what they have learned in their own words, and finally refine through teacher feedback. Memorisation is the very last step — and only when it earns its place.

Concept > Recall
1 · Understand
2 · Explore
3 · Express
4 · Refine
I

Inquiry Before Answer

Lessons begin with a question, not a definition. Our teachers are trained to wait — sometimes uncomfortably long — for students to reach for their own hypothesis before any explanation arrives.

II

Concept Before Rote

A child who understands why hydrogen and oxygen bond will not need to memorise that water is H₂O. We test understanding first; recall fluency follows from there, naturally and durably.

III

Skill-First Orientation

Every subject is taught as a craft. English is reading and writing, not just grammar. Mathematics is reasoning, not just procedure. History is source-handling. We grade how students think, not only what they reproduce.

IV

Mentor & Student in Partnership

From Class VI onwards, every student has a faculty mentor who tracks them academically and pastorally for two full years. The relationship is the foundation; the marks follow it.

Policy in practice

The new framework's emphasis on competency-based learning, multilingualism and experiential pedagogy is not a poster on our staffroom wall — it is the structure of our weekly timetable. Foundational-stage children spend roughly half their day in play-based learning, as NCF 2024 prescribes; middle-school students sit for competency-based assessments alongside conventional tests; and our Class IX–XII students choose subjects across the three traditional streams without rigid silos, in keeping with NEP 2020's flexibility principle. Our internal teacher development calendar runs on these mandates; visiting CBSE inspectors are welcome, every term.

Academic Support & Student Success

No child is left to struggle quietly.

Strong schools do not just produce toppers — they catch the child who needs an extra hour after class, and gently push the one who could go further. Here is how we do both, in the open, with the parent on board.

R

Remedial & Catch-up classes

Subject-wise small-group sessions held twice a week after school, free of cost, for students whose internal assessments suggest they need a steadier pace. Attendance is invited, never punitive — and is reviewed each term with the parent and the class teacher.

Who: Classes IV – XII · By teacher recommendation
G

Gifted-student enrichment

Saturday morning enrichment for students excelling in Maths, Science, Languages or the Humanities — Olympiad preparation, advanced reading lists, supervised research projects, and visiting-scholar masterclasses each quarter.

Who: Classes VI – XII · By teacher nomination
M

One-on-one mentor cadence

Every student from Class VI is assigned a faculty mentor who meets them privately for 20 minutes once a fortnight in the middle school, and once a week through Classes XI–XII. Notes from these conversations stay confidential within the academic team.

Cadence: Fortnightly (VI–X) · Weekly (XI–XII)
P

Pre-board & mock-exam cycle

For Class X and XII, three full pre-board cycles are conducted — September, December and January — each one in board-exact format, paper pattern and timing. Detailed item-analysis is shared with both student and parent within ten days of each cycle.

Where: Classes X & XII · 3 cycles per year
C

Parent communication rhythm

Two formal Parent–Teacher meetings per term, monthly digital progress notes via the SPS Parent Portal, and a 24-hour reply commitment on email from class teachers. Report cards include both grades and a written paragraph from each subject teacher.

Cadence: 2 PTMs / term · Monthly digital updates
W

Wellbeing & counselling

Two full-time counsellors on each campus — accessible to children at any time, on a walk-in basis, with parent involvement only where appropriate and consented. A weekly social-emotional learning class is timetabled for every grade up to Class IX.

Walk-in · Confidential · Free

Academic integrity, plainly stated.

SPS Honour Code

We expect honest work — in classrooms, in homework, in laboratories and in board examinations. Plagiarism, exam misconduct and AI-generated submissions presented as one's own are taken seriously and addressed first as a teaching moment, then if recurring, through a published disciplinary process. The intent is always to grow a child's character; the consequence is always proportionate.

Signature Academic Programs

Five things you'll only find at SPS.

Built carefully over the years, these programmes are how we go beyond the CBSE syllabus without abandoning it.

01

The SPS Olympiad Track

Class V – XII · Maths · Science · English

Identified through internal screening tests each August, students opt-in to a structured year-long preparation cycle for SOF, NSO, IMO, NSEC and English Olympiad. Sessions are taught by our subject heads, supplemented by visiting trainers from Delhi twice a term — never at the cost of school work.

What students gain
A taste for problems that don't have neat one-mark answers — and a record of national-level performance that strengthens college applications.
02

AI & Robotics Elective Track

Class VIII – XII · Project-based

The AI Lab is not a cupboard of kits — it is a working studio with a 3D printer, soldering bay, microcontrollers and a small server cluster. Students complete one capstone project a year, supervised by faculty trained at IIT Roorkee, and the senior team competes annually at WRO and Atal Tinkering Marathons.

What students gain
A working portfolio of built things — and the confidence to say "I made this" in any college interview.
03

Reading & Research Programme

Class III – XII · Library-led

The SPS Library is staffed by two full-time librarians and runs a graded reading challenge every term, an annual book fair with regional publishers, and at least two author visits a year. From Class VIII onwards, every student undertakes one long-form research project annually — properly cited, peer-reviewed and bound.

What students gain
A reading habit they take with them — and the rare ability to write a real research paper before college.
04

Career Counselling & College Readiness

Class XI – XII · 1:1 cadence

Two qualified counsellors guide students through stream selection (end of Class X), aptitude profiling (start of Class XI) and the full application cycle for JEE, NEET, CLAT, CUET, and overseas applications (UCAS, Common App). Mock interviews, SOP feedback and parent-included planning sessions are built into the schedule.

What students gain
Clarity about what comes after Class XII — and a plan, written down, that the family can hold onto.
05

Environmental & Community Learning

All grades · Curriculum-linked

Our setting is the syllabus. Students study river systems on the Kosi, glacial geomorphology at Pindari, and Kumaoni folk-architecture in the village of Kasar Devi. Class IX onwards, each student commits to 30 hours of community service annually — with a partner village school, an Almora animal shelter, or a heritage-restoration trust.

What students gain
An education rooted in a place — and the habit of showing up for a community beyond their own.

Coming on a Campus Visit?

Five things to ask the academics team.

We mean these as honest prompts for the conversation — not gotchas. Any school worth your child's eleven years should answer each of them clearly.

Schedule a visit
  1. What does a regular Tuesday look like in Class IV (or my child's grade)? Don't ask about the philosophy — ask about the timetable.
  2. How are children who fall behind academically supported, with a specific example? The answer should be unhesitating and specific.
  3. What is your stance on outside tuition for board classes? SPS's view: it should not be necessary. Hear ours; hear theirs.
  4. How is parent-teacher communication handled outside of formal PTMs? Look for cadence, not promises.
  5. Show me last year's Class XII results, line by line — not just the average. A confident school will offer this before you ask.
Academic FAQ

The questions parents ask most often.

Brief, plain answers. If something is missing here, the school office will pick up the phone — Mon–Sat, 8:30 am – 2:30 pm, on 05962 236611.

How much homework does my child get each evening?

By design, never more than 30 minutes a day in Classes I–III, an hour in IV–V, ninety minutes in VI–VIII, and roughly two hours in IX–XII. Weekend homework is restricted to project work and reading. If your child is regularly exceeding this, please tell the class teacher — that is information we want.

Will my child need outside tuition or coaching to do well in board exams?

It should not be necessary. Our 1:20 ratio, in-school remedial support, three pre-board cycles and Saturday concept clinics are designed precisely so that the school is enough. If a family chooses additional outside coaching, we ask them to share that with the class teacher so the schedules can be balanced — but we do not require, recommend, or partner with outside coaching institutes.

Can I see real Class X / XII results, not just averages?

Yes — on appointment with the academics office, we share the full anonymised result-strip for each of the last five sessions, along with subject-wise distribution. We do not publish topper photos online, but we are happy to walk parents through the numbers in detail.

What is the language of instruction?

English is the medium of instruction across all subjects, with Hindi as a compulsory second language up to Class X and an option at Class XI–XII. Classes Nursery–II are deliberately bilingual — children move between English and Hindi through the day, with Hindi storytelling protected as a daily practice. Sanskrit, French and Kumaoni Studies are offered as third-language electives in middle school.

What is the school's policy on phones and digital devices?

Personal mobile phones are not permitted on campus from Nursery to Class X. Senior students (XI–XII) may carry a phone but must deposit it at the start of the day. Tablets and laptops are issued by the school for specific assignments and used inside the classroom under supervision. Parents can reach a child during the day through the school office; we will fetch them.

How are reports and progress shared with parents?

Two formal Parent–Teacher meetings per term, plus monthly digital progress notes through the SPS Parent Portal. Term reports include numerical grades, written comments from each subject teacher, and a separate "habits and effort" rating that is not part of the academic average. Class teachers commit to replying to parent emails within one working day.

How do you approach AI and ChatGPT in academic work?

We teach with it, not against it. From Class VIII, students learn to use AI tools transparently, with citation. Submitting AI-generated work as one's own remains a violation of our honour code; using AI to brainstorm, summarise or check is permitted and supervised. Class XII students attend a dedicated session on AI ethics each year.

How are mid-year admissions handled academically?

A short diagnostic is conducted in English and Mathematics on admission. Where gaps are identified, the academics office prepares a four-to-six-week catch-up plan, often including the remedial track and a designated faculty mentor. We aim, in every case, to have a mid-year admit caught up to the class within one term — and we keep parents updated weekly.
